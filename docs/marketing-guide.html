Playable Ad Guide

The Battle Editor is a no-code tool for creating "hex-grid battle" HTML5 playable ads — the kind of interactive mini-game ad where a user fights enemies on a grid, casts spells, and gets directed to the App/Play Store at the end.

This guide explains, in plain terms, every option available when building a playable — so creative/marketing can plan a brief, and anyone can self-serve a new variant without touching code.

What is this tool?

It's a visual editor with one screen per topic (Units, Spells, Scenario, etc.) and a live preview on the right that shows exactly what the final ad will look and play like — including landscape and portrait orientations. When you're happy with it, you export ready-to-upload files for each ad network.

How a playable gets made

1
Pick characters
Set up your hero(es) and enemies — art, stats, and where they stand on the battlefield.
2
Choose a scenario
Decide how the battle plays out: a guided "puzzle" with one correct solution, or a back-and-forth "alternating" fight.
3

Optional spellbook, attack-type icons, sound effects and music.
4

Victory and defeat popups, app icon, and store links/CTA.
5

Test it live in landscape and portrait, restart anytime.
6
Export
Generate ready files for Facebook, Google Ads, Unity/AppLovin and Mintegral.

Characters (Units)

A battle has 1–6 player-side units and 1–6 enemy units, placed on a 5×4 hex grid. Each unit is fully customizable:

OptionWhat it controls
NameInternal label only (not shown to players) — useful for keeping track of units in the editor.
Type — Melee / Ranged / FlyingDetermines how the unit behaves in battle: melee must walk up to attack, ranged can attack from a distance, flying can move and attack anywhere on the board.
HP, Damage, Defense, Damage MultiplierCombat stats — how much health it has, how hard it hits, how much incoming damage it blocks, and a multiplier for fine-tuning balance.
Grid position (column/row)Where the unit starts on the battlefield.
Display sizeHow large the character's artwork appears.
Move rangeHow many hexes the unit can move per turn.
Elemental resistance (Fire / Ice)Makes a unit immune/resistant to a specific spell element — useful for puzzle scenarios ("you can't beat this enemy with the wrong spell").
Flip horizontallyMirrors the artwork — handy for making the same sprite face left or right.
Idle & Attack artworkTwo images per unit: a resting pose and an attacking pose, used to animate combat.

Add or remove units freely (minimum 1 per side, maximum 6 per side).

Hero Portraits

Two optional decorative character portraits — Left Hero and Right Hero — that frame the battle scene (e.g. your game's main characters watching the fight). For each one you can set:

  • The portrait artwork
  • Its position on screen (X/Y)
  • Whether it's flipped horizontally

This section also controls the battlefield grid's vertical position on screen, separately for landscape and portrait — useful for fitting the grid around your background art.

Spells & Spellbook

An optional "spellbook" gives the player access to up to 2 spells (e.g. Fireball, Ice Shard) that can be cast at enemies during the battle.

OptionWhat it controls
Include Spellbook (on/off)Turns the entire spell system on or off. If off, the battle is pure melee/ranged combat with no spell UI.
Spell name & element (Fire/Ice)Each spell has a theme — and enemies can be made resistant to a specific element (see Characters).
Spell artwork (projectile)The visual that flies from the caster to the target.
Cast & impact sound effectsSeparate sounds for "spell launched" and "spell hits target".
Spellbook open/closed iconsThe button artwork players tap to open/close the spell menu.
Attack-type icons (Melee/Ranged/Flying)Small icons that briefly appear over a unit to signal what kind of attack it's performing — purely cosmetic, all optional.

Battle Scenarios

This is the "gameplay design" section — it determines what the player actually has to do to win, and what happens when they make a mistake. There are two modes:

Puzzle Mode guided

The player must perform a specific sequence of actions to win — e.g. "cast Fireball on the Valkyrie, then melee-attack the Armored Giant." This is ideal for showcasing a specific mechanic from your game ("only fire beats this enemy") and creating an "aha, I figured it out" moment.

  • Winning sequence — the exact ordered steps (which unit, which action — spell/melee/ranged, which target) that lead to victory.
  • Fail conditions & hints — up to 4 specific mistakes a player can make (e.g. "moved toward the flying enemy", "killed the wrong enemy first", "used the wrong spell element", "wasted a spell"). Each shows a custom multi-line hint to nudge the player toward the right move.
  • Retaliation — when a specific enemy dies, another enemy can "retaliate" with a scripted counter-attack and dialogue line, adding drama before the final blow.

Alternating Mode back-and-forth

A more traditional turn-based fight: player turn, then enemy turn, repeat until one side is defeated. Good for a more "real game" feel.

  • Who goes first — player or enemy.
  • Player turn order — if you have multiple player units, define which one acts each turn (they can repeat/cycle).
  • Enemy turn script — for each enemy turn, define the attacker, whether it attacks or moves, the target, damage dealt, a speech/dialogue line, and (for moves) where it moves to.
  • Attack reactions — per enemy, decide whether being attacked triggers an automatic retaliation, with its own damage amount and dialogue line.

In both modes, the same fail-condition hint texts can be used to guide the player back on track, and the same retaliation system can add personality to the fight.

End Screens & Call-to-Action

What the player sees when the battle ends — this is where the conversion happens.

OptionWhat it controls
App iconYour game's icon, shown on both the victory and defeat screens.
Victory popupBanner image, board/background image, and a "Play Now" / CTA button image shown when the player wins.
Defeat popupBanner, board, a "Retry" button, a "Store" button, and the color of the hint text shown on failure.
Store linksThe iOS App Store and Google Play URLs the CTA buttons open.
"Show store button after N fails"Lets the player retry a few times before the install prompt appears — balances "let them enjoy the game" vs. "drive installs".

OptionWhat it controls
Landscape backgroundFull-screen artwork behind the battle, used when the device is in landscape orientation.
Portrait backgroundSeparate artwork for portrait orientation — the editor's live preview lets you check both.
Walkable hex tileThe graphic shown for grid cells the player can move to.

Music & Sound

Full audio customization — every sound in the playable can be swapped:

  • Background music — looping track for the whole experience.
  • UI sounds — spellbook open, spell select, walk, grid select.
  • Spell sounds — separate "cast" and "hit" sounds for each of the two spells.
  • Combat sounds — distinct attack & death sounds for the player and for each enemy type (melee, ranged, flying).
  • Fail sound — plays when the player loses.

Note: for Facebook exports, audio is automatically removed to meet that network's strict 2 MB file-size limit (see Exporting below).

Text & Layout Fine-Tuning

For polishing how on-screen text appears in each orientation:

  • Hint text (shown on failure) — vertical position and font size, set separately for landscape and portrait.
  • Speech bubble (dialogue during battle) — horizontal/vertical position and font size, set separately for landscape and portrait.

Asset Library

Every image or sound you upload can be saved (💾) to a personal Library tab. From then on, any upload slot anywhere in the tool can pull that same asset from the library instead of re-uploading — great for reusing a character, icon set, or music track across multiple playable variants.

Templates & Saved Configs

Two ways to save and reuse a full playable setup:

  • Templates — save the current setup with a name for the rest of your session, and reload it instantly. Great for quickly switching between a few variants while iterating.
  • Export/Import as file — save the entire configuration as a single file you can send to a teammate, archive, or re-load later to continue editing — a full backup of everything: characters, scenario, art, audio, and text settings.

This makes it easy to produce several creative variants (different art style, different scenario, different CTA) from one base setup without starting from scratch.

Live Preview

The right-hand panel always shows a fully playable preview of the current setup — not a static mockup. You can:

  • Play through the actual battle exactly as a user would.
  • Toggle between landscape and portrait to check both orientations.
  • Hit Restart to replay from the beginning after any change.
  • Undo / Redo any edit from the header at any time.

Exporting for Ad Networks

One click generates ready-to-upload files for all major playable ad networks. Each network has its own size limit and file format — the export screen shows the size of your current playable against each limit, with a ✓ or ⚠ warning if it's over budget.

NetworkFile formatSize limitNotes
FacebookSingle .html file2 MBAudio is automatically stripped out to help meet this tighter limit.
Google Ads.zip (containing index.html)5 MBIncludes audio.
Unity / AppLovinSingle .html file5 MBIncludes audio; this is also the format used for the in-editor Live Preview.
Mintegral.zip (index.html + main.js)5 MBIncludes audio; code is split into two files as required by Mintegral.

You can download one network at a time or use "Download All" to get all four builds in one go.

Example playable setups

A few ways the building blocks above can be combined into different creative briefs:

"Spot the trick" puzzle ad

Puzzle mode, spellbook on, one enemy resistant to one element. The player must discover the correct spell before attacking — great for highlighting an RPG's elemental mechanics. Use fail-condition hints to teach the rule if they get it wrong.

"Epic showdown" turn-based ad

Alternating mode with multiple player and enemy units, scripted enemy dialogue each turn, and attack reactions for dramatic counter-attacks — feels closer to the real game's combat loop, good for action/strategy titles.

"Quick win" minimal ad

Spellbook off, 1 player unit vs. 1 enemy, puzzle mode with a 1-step winning sequence (single attack). Fast, simple, and lightweight — easiest to fit Facebook's 2 MB limit without dropping much art.

Branded variant via Templates

Build one solid base setup, save it as a Template, then duplicate it and swap only the backgrounds, hero portraits, app icon, and store links to quickly produce localized or seasonal variants from the same gameplay.

Pre-export checklist

  • Every unit has both Idle and Attack artwork uploaded.
  • Scenario mode chosen, and winning sequence / enemy turns make sense in the Live Preview.
  • Fail-condition hints are written and helpful (puzzle mode).
  • Victory and defeat popups have banner, board and button art, and the app icon is set.
  • Store URLs (iOS & Android) are correct and point to the right app listing.
  • Music and key sound effects are uploaded (remember Facebook strips audio).
  • Checked the Live Preview in both landscape and portrait.
  • Checked file sizes in the Export screen — no ⚠ warnings for your target networks.
  • Saved a Template or exported the JSON config as a backup before exporting.
